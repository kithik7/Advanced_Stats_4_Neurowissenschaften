2) Why are many, but not all, red symbols below both criteria lines?

The non-null distribution and the null db both have Sd = 1, and a gap of 3 SD is not enough to
fully separate them, their tails still overlap. So a non-null sample could land on a smaller value that looks 
statistically not so great giving it a p-value above the threshold. That sample, is a false negative, 
a real effect that random variation did not show. Similar to type 2 errors from before 

3)  does the false discovery fraction match q?

At N=1000, only 13 samples were rejected by the B-H procedure, thi
reliably show the target rate and by chance zero of them were false
discoveries. 
At N=20000, 305 samples were rejected, and 61 of those were
actually false , giving me an observed fraction of 0.200,
matching the target q=0.2 almost exactly. 

This confirms that Benjamini-Hochberg test does not promise zero false discoveries, it reliably gives you the average
proportion of false discoveries which stays close to q, and this only works with a large enough sample size
