Questions and Answers from the Exercise 
1) judging by eye, which curve fits better?
At N = 30 the line and the parabola sort of overlap, and the scatter of
points makes it hard to distinguish between them. 
At N = 500 the parabola bends to follow the data at the low and high ends of x, while the line
cuts across the curve instead of following it. So increasing N does actually
make a difference

2) from the standard deviation of residuals, which is better?

The parabola has the lower residual std at every N tested, so it is better fit throughout, even at N = 30 where
I could not tell it apart.

3) Which method is more sensitive, judging by eye or standard deviation?
I believe standard deviation is more sensitive. Even at N = 30, where the two curves
looked almost identical by eye, the residual std and the explained/residual
ratio is what truly showed that the parabola was ahead. Numbers in this case tell you differences that 
one couldn't by visually interpreting the graph (by eye).

4) Why might eye and objective measures disagree?
Through a scatter of points, the shape of a line or a parabola is rather easy to confuse.
The std is computed from every single point's distance to the curve accurately, so it is more sensitive
and picks up those small differences that are lost in visual interpretation. Objective measures rely on numbers and tell you
information about the minutiae while by eye, we are able to intepret only on the basis of the observable shape that is available
to us.
